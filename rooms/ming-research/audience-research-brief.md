# Work order: how the audience actually talks about this

Owner: Dev. Reviewer: Mira. Start immediately; this brief is complete.

## Why this matters

zhihao feng is building MING, a consumer app that turns a person's birth date, time, and location into a Four Pillars (BaZi) chart and explains it in plain contemporary language across three areas: understanding yourself, understanding your timing (the personal "season" you are in), and understanding relationships through chart-to-chart comparison. MING has no website and no launched copy yet, so there is no existing voice to defend — the words come from whatever this research finds.

The target audience, as stated by the founder: English-speaking, astrology-curious people roughly 22-38 who already use Co-Star, The Pattern, CHANI, Human Design, Enneagram, attachment styles, or journaling apps, and who know little or no BaZi. Secondary: Chinese diaspora and mixed-culture users whose family mentioned BaZi but nobody ever explained it in a way that felt relevant.

## Deliverable

A workspace file in this room that answers, in the audience's own words:

- How these people describe what they get out of astrology and personality systems — quote them verbatim, with a link to each source post or comment.
- What they complain about in Co-Star, The Pattern, CHANI, and Human Design. Specific complaints, not themes.
- Every time BaZi, Four Pillars, or Chinese astrology comes up in these English-language spaces: what do people already believe, what confuses them, what do they wish someone explained?
- What the Chinese diaspora segment says about family-mentioned BaZi they never had explained.
- A closing section of concrete implications: phrases MING should use, phrases MING should never use, and the two or three hooks most likely to land — each tied to the evidence above.

Look at Reddit, TikTok, and app reviews. Real posts and real comments, dated.
