# MING landing page — brand & conversion review

Reviewed: draft `0880663d5bc4`, preview `a0cb7c3d1b63`, at 1280×900 and 390×844.
Reviewer: Nora. Scope: voice, positioning, conversion clarity, mobile layout, identity strength.

## Verdict

The copy is genuinely good and mostly clears the bar. The identity does not yet.

Cover the MING wordmark and this page could be a therapy practice, a journaling app, a literary
newsletter, or a natural wine importer. It is warm-paper editorial — a well-executed *category*
look, not an identity. Nothing on the page comes from the system the product is actually about.
That is the one structural failure; everything else is line editing.

---

## 1. Voice: mostly smart friend, drifts oracular in three specific places

### 1.1 No contractions, anywhere

The page systematically avoids them:

| Current | Should be |
| --- | --- |
| "MING never tells you what is going to happen." | keep as-is — see note below |
| "That is the whole difference between a reading and a fortune." | keep as-is — see note below |
| "in language you would actually use" | "in language you'd actually use" |
| "the theme that is currently live" | "one theme that's live right now" |
| "a decision you have already made" | "a decision you've already made" |
| "you will hear once, when it is" | "we'll write once, the day it opens" |

Uncontracted English reads formal and literary. Combined with italic serif sample lines, it tips
toward scripture — the exact register we said we are not. Contract everywhere **except** the pine
manifesto band, where the formality is load-bearing and earns itself.

### 1.2 The italic serif treatment of sample lines

Italic + display serif + pull-quote rule is the visual grammar of an oracle. The lines themselves
are smart-friend; the typesetting is horoscope. Set them in the body face at a slightly larger
size, keep the terracotta rule. Same words, register fixed, no copy change needed.

### 1.3 "EXAMPLE LINE" caption

Spec language, and it sits *below* the quote so it reads as an apology after the fact. Move it
above as a lead-in: `IT MIGHT SAY` or `WHAT YOU'D ACTUALLY READ`.

## 2. The first five seconds have a comprehension gap

Headline says **Four Pillars**. Subhead says **BaZi**. Nothing tells the reader they are the same
thing. For the primary audience — astrology-curious, little or no BaZi — that is two unknown
proper nouns in eight seconds with no bridge.

**Current subhead:**

> MING takes your birth date, time and place, builds your BaZi chart — an old Chinese way of
> reading the conditions a person is working with — and writes it out in plain English. No
> Heavenly Stems on the first screen. No horoscope voice.

Three problems: the two names are never linked; "the conditions **a person** is working with" is
third person and distancing in a sentence that is otherwise about *you*; and "No Heavenly Stems on
the first screen" reassures against a term the reader has never heard, so it lands as noise and
faintly promises jargon later.

**Proposed:**

> Your birth date, time and place build a BaZi chart — Four Pillars — an old Chinese way of
> reading the conditions you're working with. MING writes it out in plain English: no jargon on
> the first screen, no horoscope voice.

### Headline

"Your Four Pillars chart, in language you would actually use." sells the *format* — a translated
chart — rather than the payoff. It survives because "Four Pillars" is the differentiator, so I'd
keep the structure and fix the contraction: **"Your Four Pillars chart, in language you'd actually
use."** Then let the subhead and the band carry the payoff. Worth A/B-ing against a payoff-first
alternative later, but not worth blocking on now.

---

## 3. The best positioning line on the page is buried

Section 02 body contains this, mid-paragraph:

> This is the part Western astrology never really gave you.

That is the sharpest competitive sentence anywhere on the page, and it is in the middle of the
third paragraph of the third section. Pull it up — as a section eyebrow, or higher still. Also cut
the hedge: "never really gave you" → **"never gave you."** Two softeners cancelling each other.

Related conversion gap: nothing high on the page tells a reader who already uses Co-Star or CHANI
why this is different. One comparison line above the fold will move signups more than any other
single change here.

## 4. Line-level flags

| Location | Current | Issue | Proposed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero H1 | "in language you would actually use" | stiff, no contraction | "in language you'd actually use" |
| Hero sub | "the conditions a person is working with" | third person in a second-person sentence | "the conditions you're working with" |
| Hero sub | "No Heavenly Stems on the first screen." | reassures against an unknown term | "no jargon on the first screen" |
| Section 02 | "conditions favour starting" | British spelling | "favor" |
| Section 02 | "Western astrology never really gave you" | hedged; buried | "never gave you"; promote it |
| Ritual intro | "one observation, the theme that is currently live, one thing worth doing, and one question" | four items, three grammars | "one observation, one theme that's live right now, one thing worth doing, one question to sit with" |
| Ritual card | `THE THEME THAT'S LIVE` | breaks the ONE-x label pattern; also the only contraction on the page | `ONE LIVE THEME` |
| Ritual card | "Conditions favour closing loops" | British spelling | "favor" |
| Final CTA | "Leave your email and you will hear once, when it is." | tangled trailing clause | "Leave your email and we'll write once, the day it opens." |
| Final CTA | "this is for you too" | makes a whole secondary audience an afterthought | "…nobody ever explained it in a way that meant anything, MING is the explanation." |
| Footer / header | "FOUR PILLARS, PLAINLY" vs "Conditions and choices, not predictions." | two taglines | defensible split — descriptor up top, boundary at the bottom. Lock "Four Pillars, plainly" as the canonical descriptor everywhere else. |

**Locale:** "favour" appears twice. The named comparison set (Co-Star, The Pattern, CHANI) is
US-centric and so is the primary audience. Go US spelling and lock it in the brand system so this
does not drift file by file.

## 5. Lines to protect

Do not let these get edited away:

- "MING never tells you what is going to happen." — best line on the page. The negation defines the
  brand better than any positive claim could.
- "Then MING gets out of the way."
- "You think out loud; they need to go quiet first. Same intention, different recovery speed."
- "Where are you waiting for certainty about a decision you have already made?"

The relationships sample line is the strongest proof of the whole product thesis and it is in the
third of three sections. Consider it as hero-adjacent proof.

---

## 6. Conversion clarity — this part is good

Verified at 390×844, no scroll: wordmark, headline, subhead, email field, full-width button, and
the "One email when MING opens. Nothing else." reassurance all sit above the fold. Full-width
button, real tap target, single field, no competing action anywhere on the page. That is the best
thing about this build and it should not be traded away for anything.

Duplicate capture at the bottom with identical label and reassurance line — correct.

Deliberately absent and should stay absent: waitlist counter, social proof, launch countdown. A
fake-feeling counter would cost more trust than it buys signups, and the page's whole argument is
that it does not overclaim.

---

## 7. Identity: the structural problem

What is working, and is not accidental: no mystical gradients, no starfield, no glassmorphic
cards, no SaaS feature grid, no stock photography, no imagery at all. That restraint is real and
it clears both failure modes we named.

What is missing: an ownable visual device. Right now the display serif carries 100% of the
identity, and it is a fairly familiar high-contrast serif. Warm paper, deep pine, one terracotta
accent, editorial serif — that is a current, tasteful, borrowed look. A stranger would not
recognise it twice.

**Recommendation — the pillars as structure, not decoration.** Four is in the name and it is
already half-present by accident: the ritual card is four stacked items. Make a four-part
structural rhythm the recurring device — a four-column measure in the hero, four-beat section
spacing, the ritual card's four items as the canonical expression of it. Structural, survives a
phone, costs nothing in load, and comes from the actual system rather than being applied to it.

**Considered and rejected:** a five-element accent palette (wood/fire/earth/metal/water each
owning a colour). It is the obvious move, and it is the one that drags us straight back toward
generic mystical. Keep the single terracotta.

**Also worth one round:** test a less-common display face before locking type. The current serif is
doing all the differentiating work and it is not distinctive enough to carry that alone.

---

## Priority order

1. Fix the Four Pillars / BaZi bridge in the subhead — comprehension, costs nothing.
2. Contractions throughout except the pine band; sample lines out of italic serif.
3. Promote the "Western astrology never gave you" line; add one comparison line above the fold.
4. Parallel the four ritual items and their labels; fix "favour" → "favor".
5. Introduce the four-part structural motif.
6. Do not touch the mobile hero.
