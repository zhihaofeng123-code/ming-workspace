# Review: MING Identity & Voice v0.9

Dev · 2026-09-02 · reviewing [ming-identity.md](/rooms/353871fb77e1/file/a97b343243f1) and the twelve assets in [the v0.9 thread](/workspaces/fc8a8e44157c/rooms/353871fb77e1/threads/185f8ffb7ab9)

Scope: Nora asked five questions — does it land with a 22–38 Co-Star user, does it read as deeper rather than decorative, which lines are Barnum, where the voice slips, does Grain / Season / Between hold up. This document answers those and nothing else. Everything marked *inference* is mine and not sourced.

---

## Summary of the verdict

The central bet — that the churn reason is "it never got deeper" — is **correct and externally supported**. Three of the four load-bearing weaknesses below are not taste disagreements; they are the doc contradicting itself or contradicting a checkable fact.

Four things to fix before this is finalised, in priority order:

1. **The flagship line breaks the flagship rule.** The hero copy predicts in the future tense on three surfaces, including the identity board that prints the prohibition in its own footer.
2. **"Conditions, not prediction" is table stakes, not a differentiator.** Shipping BaZi apps already claim it in their store copy, verbatim.
3. **The identity is a retention story with no acquisition story.** Co-Star's growth mechanic was cruelty-as-screenshot. The doc bans the mechanic and does not replace it.
4. **"Almanac" carries prediction in the US market**, and the doc uses it as the word that proves MING does not predict.

Plus one factual error about 明 printed on a published asset, and one structural break in the colour system.

## 1. Does it land with a 22–38 Co-Star user?

### The core premise checks out

The doc claims the reader "drifted because after a year it never got deeper — it only got more clever." That is unsourced in the doc but it is externally supportable, which is worth knowing before the founder asks.

- A 2026 astrologer review of Co-Star names the same failure under its own heading, "There's No Learning Curve or Growth Path": "After a few weeks of regular use, most Co-Star users have seen essentially everything the app offers... It's a loop rather than a journey," and concludes "Co-Star excels as a gateway. Its limitation is that it doesn't grow with you." ([Aurae, Co-Star App Review 2026](https://www.auraeastrology.com/blog/co-star-app-review-2026-an-astrologers-honest-opinion))
- The "show the mechanism" wedge is supported by the same critique from a practising astrologer: "Real astrology tells you why something is happening. Co–Star rarely reveals its reasoning, which creates the illusion of depth without offering the substance behind it." ([chi-nese.com, 2025-07-04](https://chi-nese.com/is-co-star-accurate-an-old-school-astrologers-take/))
- The warmth position is also supported. Co-Star's own founder confirmed the app will "troll you a bit," and the criticism that followed is about emotional cost, not tone preference: an astrologer reports "clients come in genuinely worried after reading one of Co-Star's more dramatic daily notifications." ([VALLEY Magazine](https://www.valleymagazinepsu.com/its-about-time-to-delete-co-star/); [chi-nese.com](https://chi-nese.com/is-co-star-accurate-an-old-school-astrologers-take/))

So: mechanism + warmth is a defensible wedge. The doc is right about the gap. It is wrong about how big a moat that gap is — see §2.

### The thing the doc does not have an answer for

Co-Star did not spread because it was deep. It spread because it was screenshottable, and specifically because it was *rude*.

- The push notifications became a documented meme format on Twitter from 2018 onward, with the Know Your Meme entry marked Confirmed. ([Know Your Meme, Co-Star Notifications](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/co-star-notifications))
- The pleasure users describe is explicitly masochistic and explicitly social: "I get a little thrill at the prospect of the app dragging me for filth," in a piece subtitled "CO-STAR didn't come here to make friends." ([The Outline, 2019-04-18](https://theoutline.com/post/7326/my-astrology-app-hates-me-and-i-can-t-get-enough))
- Contemporary coverage is a list of screenshots users chose to publish. ([Jezebel, 2019-04-02](https://www.jezebel.com/why-are-co-stars-daily-notifications-so-rude-1833747335); [Teen Vogue, 2019-03-29](https://www.teenvogue.com/story/astrology-app-co-star-bizarre-alerts))

MING's voice section bans the entire mechanism — no cruelty, no deadpan, no aphorism, "never impressed with itself." Fine as a values call. But the identity then has no stated answer to *why anyone posts a MING screenshot*, and for a product whose invite loop is chart comparison, that is an identity-level hole, not a growth-team problem.

The good news is that the answer is already in the doc and undeclared: **the personal eight-square mark**. It is the only object here that is simultaneously unique per user, instantly recognisable as MING, legible at 88px, and meaningless to anyone who has not downloaded the app. That is a profile-picture asset and an export asset, and §6 currently treats it as an internal system note. *Inference, but a cheap one:* promote the personal mark to a named, spec'd share surface with its own export rules, or accept that MING has no organic distribution mechanic in the brand.

### The other thing the doc does not have an answer for: who is behind the reading

Co-Star's most-repeated credibility attack is staffing: "The Co-Star team has no astrologers on their team, so it is unclear where they get their information." ([VALLEY Magazine](https://www.valleymagazinepsu.com/its-about-time-to-delete-co-star/))

MING's entire claim is *we show the mechanism*. A brand that stakes itself on mechanism and cannot show provenance is more exposed to this attack than Co-Star was, not less — and the secondary audience is precisely the people who can call it, because they have a relative who knows. The identity needs a provenance element: a stated calculation basis, a named human reading, or a visible "here is how this was derived" surface. Its absence from a 19KB identity doc is conspicuous.

## 2. Does it read as deeper rather than decorative?

Yes, in the form. The five phases as a semantic data layer is real, the mono marginalia genuinely reads as a record rather than a magazine, and the solar-term date stamp (`白露 · WHITE DEW · DAY 3`) is the strongest single element in the system — it teaches while it decorates, which is the definition of not-decorative.

The chart on `07-product-screens.jpg` is also **correct**, which matters more than it sounds: 庚 Yang Metal / 辰 Earth, 壬 Yang Water / 申 Metal, 癸 Water / 酉 Metal, 己 Earth / 巳 Fire, and the reading "Your day pillar is Yang Water on Metal" all check out against the stem and branch elements. A single wrong character here would have cost the whole positioning. Keep whatever process produced that.

But "deeper" is doing two jobs in Nora's question — deeper than Co-Star, and deeper than the other people already doing this. The doc only answers the first.

### The differentiator is already claimed by shipping competitors

The doc's hard constraint — conditions, tendencies, effort, never fate or prediction — is currently the default marketing register of the English-language BaZi category, not an opening.

| Competitor | Their own store/site copy | Overlap with MING |
|---|---|---|
| [My BaZi Astrology (App Store)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/my-bazi-astrology/id6758286066) | "Effort-based language, not fate or predictions"; "Non-judgmental and practical" | MING's stated hard constraint, verbatim |
| [Jiazi — Bazi & Saju Calculator (Google Play)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en&id=com.hexagramdreams.bazi) | "read what it actually means... without the jargon"; "what it asks of you and where the friction shows up" | MING's register *and* the house word "friction" |
| [Meta BaZi](https://www.metabazi.com/) | "Honest note: BaZi reveals tendencies and timing, not certainties. It won't tell you lottery numbers or guarantee outcomes." | MING's "tends to, never will" |
| [Bazi Compass (App Store)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bazi-compass/id6760332047) | "Your Bazi Birth Chart, Decoded"; "See where you strengthen each other and where friction may arise" | MING's Between framing |

Worth noting these are mostly weak products — My BaZi still ships "DAILY THREE-TIER LUCK... favorable colors, and best direction," which is straight fortune-telling under a disclaimer ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/my-bazi-astrology/id6758286066)). So the opportunity is real. But the *claim* is not the moat, because four competitors already make it. The moat is craft — the paper, the type, the semantic palette, the correct chart — and the doc should say that out loud rather than resting on "we don't predict," which reads as a differentiator only to someone who has not opened the App Store.

*Inference:* this also means "no BaZi competition" is not a safe assumption for the landing page or the founder deck.

### "Almanac" points the wrong way in this market

The doc's load-bearing move is that an almanac is "structurally the opposite of fortune-telling." In the US, for an English-speaking audience, the reference almanac is The Old Farmer's Almanac, and its central product is long-range prediction with an advertised hit rate: "On average, we correctly predict the direction of seasonal temperature and precipitation departures about 80% of the time." ([almanac.com](https://www.almanac.com/how-accurate-old-farmers-almanacs-weather-forecast))

That accuracy claim is also contested — an independent five-year study found roughly 51% of monthly temperature and precipitation predictions correct, "similar to chance" ([cleveland.com, 2026-07-22](https://www.cleveland.com/metro/2026/07/how-accurate-is-the-old-farmers-almanac-independent-study-found-about-51-accuracy.html)); a University of Illinois study put the competing Farmers' Almanac at about 52% ([Family Handyman](https://www.familyhandyman.com/article/farmers-almanac-accuracy/)). And the older almanac's method is described as drawing on "historical weather patterns in addition to factors like sunspot activity and even astrology" ([same source](https://www.familyhandyman.com/article/farmers-almanac-accuracy/)).

So the word MING picks to prove it is not a fortune-teller denotes, in this market: a folksy old book that makes contested long-range predictions partly from astrology.

I do not think this kills the metaphor — it is still the right *shape*, and "an almanac for a person" is a good line. But the doc currently treats "almanac = calendar = not prediction" as self-evident, and it is not; it is a meaning MING has to actively teach. Recommend the doc name the risk and specify the teaching move, rather than leaving it as an assumed win.

## 3. The Barnum lines

Applying the doc's own test #2 — "could this line be pasted into any other astrology app unchanged?"

**The worst one, because it is a template used twice.**

> "Whether that shows up as leadership or as burnout depends mostly on what you put around it." (§8 example 3)
> "Whether that reads as composure or as distance depends mostly on what you put around it." (`07-product-screens.jpg`, Grain panel)

Same sentence, two nouns swapped. It is a two-sided, unfalsifiable statement — the classic Forer construction — and it is unfalsifiable *by design*, because whichever way the user's life went, the line was right. Worse than being Barnum: a user who sees the template twice learns that the mechanism is a mad-lib, and the entire "we show the reason" position dies in that moment. This is the single most damaging line in the doc, because it appears in the flagship example *and* in the product screen.

**Straight Forer pairs.**

> "Left alone it burns hot and public: good at starting, bad at maintaining." (§8 example 3)

"Good at starting, bad at maintaining" is a stock dual-trait ascription. Paste it into any app.

> "Slow to say what you actually want." (`07-product-screens.jpg`)

Near-universally endorsable.

**Statements that presume facts about the user's life the system cannot know.**

> "Three heavy weeks in a row." (§8 example 6, and the hero of `04-card-season.jpg`)

The system does not know the last three weeks were heavy. If they were, the user credits the app with knowledge it does not have. If they were not, the copy is priming them to reinterpret them as heavy. Both outcomes are the mechanism the brand says it is replacing. This is the biggest asset in the set and it is built on an assertion about the user's interior life.

> "Have the one you've been putting off." (§8 example 2)

Presumes a deferred conversation. Everyone has one, which is what makes it Barnum.

> "That's the thing you both quietly want to be in charge of." (§8 example 4, and `05-card-between.jpg`)

True of nearly any two people. The mechanism (Earth branch clash) is stated, but the *interpretation* attached to it is generic.

**One that is not Barnum but is worse — it is the banned category with a Chinese name.**

> "This is a Wealth month for you. It doesn't hand you money — it makes money-shaped conversations more likely to go somewhere." (§8 example 2)

"More likely to go somewhere" is a probability claim about future events, which is what a prediction is. And the doc bans "lucky day" while keeping "Wealth month" — the same object, relabelled. If 財 months are in the product, the no-prediction rule needs an explicit carve-out that says how MING talks about them, or the rule will be broken every time the feature ships.

**Cleanest lines in the doc, for contrast** — keep these as the calibration examples:

- "Your chart is eight characters. We'll spend a year explaining them, two minutes at a time." (Also the best line in the doc: it is the only one that sells duration instead of insight.)
- "Birth date, time, and place. Time matters most: it sets the hour pillar, and that's the one about how you actually operate."
- "White Dew, day 3. Today's note is up."
- "Nothing here until you add someone. Between works by comparison — one chart on its own has nothing to be between."

## 4. Where the voice slips

### Into prediction — and this is a printed contradiction, not a slip

> "You'll cut to the point faster than people expect, and it will land as blunt more often than it usually does."

"You'll" and "it will" are future-tense event statements. The doc bans them in §8 ("**'Tends to,' never 'will.'** No prediction of events, ever"). The identity board prints `NO "YOU WILL"` in its own footer. This line appears in **three** places: §8 example 1, the AFTER panel of `02-identity-board.jpg`, and the body of `03-card-daily-note.jpg`.

So the hero example of the correct voice, on the board that states the rule, violates the rule. That is the first thing a sharp reader will find, and it is the cheapest fix in this review: "cuts to the point faster than people expect, and it lands as blunt more often than it usually does."

### Into wellness

> "Three heavy weeks is not a character flaw." (`04-card-season.jpg`)
> "Put one warm thing in the day, and don't schedule anything after eight." (§8 example 6)

"X is not a character flaw" is reassurance-register — the you're-not-broken move, which is The Pattern's whole voice, and adjacent to the "hold space" family the doc bans. "One warm thing in the day" and a bedtime instruction are self-care prescriptions. The doc says "we are cooler and more exact than that." This asset is neither; it is the softest thing in the set, and it is the poster.

Note this is the *biggest* asset and the one most likely to be shared, so the wellness drift is not marginal — it is the brand's most public face.

### Into horoscope / Co-Star

> "A lot of edge, and nothing to cut but people." (`03-card-daily-note.jpg`)
> "Worth naming out loud before it becomes a Thursday." (`05-card-between.jpg`)

These are genuinely good lines. They are also aphoristic, mechanism-free at the point of impact, and quotable in exactly Co-Star's register — the second one is a cryptic-clever turn with no reason attached. I am not saying cut them. I am saying the doc claims MING does not do this, and the two best headlines in the asset set do it. Either the voice section admits an aphorism tier and rules when a headline may run ahead of its mechanism, or the assets get rewritten downward and lose their teeth. Pretending the tension is not there is the one option that fails.

### Into startup / the paywall contradiction

> "The free note covers your day. The full chart covers the next ten years, and it takes longer to read than two minutes."

The second half is excellent. The first half sells a ten-year forecast at the paywall, in a brand whose §2 says "Not a prediction engine." Ten-year luck pillars (大運) are a forecasting instrument. This is not a wording slip; it is an unresolved conflict between the product's actual content and the brand's hard constraint, and it surfaces at the exact moment money changes hands, which is the worst possible place for it. The doc needs a stated position on how MING sells 大運 without predicting.

## 5. Does Grain / Season / Between hold up?

**Season — yes.** Keep it. It is the real word, it maps to the 24 solar terms the product already runs on, and it is legible at zero context.

**Grain — no, on two counts.**

*Legibility.* Nav labels are the highest-frequency, lowest-context words in a product. A first-time user tapping `GRAIN` in the tab bar of `07-product-screens.jpg` has no way to know it means "you." Every other tab is guessable. "Grain" is a metaphor asked to do a wayfinding job, and metaphors are bad at wayfinding.

*System inconsistency.* Wood-grain is a **Wood** metaphor. In a five-phase system where the whole promise is that the phases carry meaning, naming the self-understanding section after one phase silently privileges it. A Metal-heavy user's "grain" is a category error inside MING's own vocabulary — and the doc is otherwise scrupulous about not letting metaphors contradict the system. This is the one place it does.

*Inference, offered as a direction not a decision:* the self section wants a word that is phase-neutral and self-explaining. "Chart" is honest and boring. "Your eight" is in-register, distinctive, and teaches the 八字 fact the mark is built on — and the product screen already says "YOUR EIGHT CHARACTERS" as a label, so it is half-adopted.

**Between — no, on collision.**

[Between](https://between.us/?lang=en) is a live couples app by VCNC that claims "Over 35 million couples," is shipping on [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=kr.co.vcnc.android.couple) and the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/between-couples-love-tracker/id458035189), and owns between.us. It is a relationship product, which is the same adjacent category as MING's relationship feature.

That is not a legal opinion — I am not qualified to give one. It is a discoverability and word-of-mouth problem: MING's Between is the feature the invite loop runs through, so it is the name users will say out loud and type into a store search, and the search result is a 35-million-couple app in the same emotional category. The word is also a preposition, which makes it near-unsearchable on its own.

The doc's reasoning for the name is good — "about the space rather than a score," avoids dragging a percentage in. That reasoning survives a different word. `05-card-between.jpg` already prints the better positioning as a header: `NO SCORE · NO PERCENTAGE · NO VERDICT`.

## 6. Two things that are just wrong

### 明 is not sun over moon

The doc says the mark's two-row geometry is "conveniently, 明: sun over moon," and `01-mark-system.jpg` prints "Sun over moon: 明" as published copy.

明 is a left–right compound. Its ideographic description sequence is ⿰日月 — 日 beside 月, never stacked. ([Wiktionary, 明](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%98%8E)) The stacked-looking variant in the oracle bone record is a different character, 朙, built from 囧 (window) + 月 ([same source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%98%8E)).

This is small and it is fatal in one specific way: it is a claim about Chinese characters, printed on a brand asset, in a brand whose entire pitch is that it is accurate about the tradition, aimed partly at diaspora users who read Chinese. It is the exact error that gets screenshotted back at you.

The fix costs nothing, because the mark does not need the pun. Stems above branches is already the correct and sufficient reason for a 4×2 grid. Change "sun over moon" to "sun and moon," keep the 明 = 日 + 月 fact (which is right and which the doc uses well elsewhere), and stop asking it to justify the geometry.

### Cinnabar cannot mean "now" and mean Fire at the same time

§4 states two rules that collide:

- 火 Fire = `--fire` `#BE4127`
- "Cinnabar `#BE4127` is the only accent, and it means 'now.'"

They are the same hex. So on any Fire day, in any Fire month, or on any chart containing a Fire stem or branch, the "you are here" signal is indistinguishable from the data layer. This is not hypothetical — it is visible in the shipped assets:

- `07-product-screens.jpg` uses cinnabar for the active nav item (now), the italic reflection question (a question), and the 巳 Fire branch (data) on the same screen. Three meanings, one colour.
- `05-card-between.jpg` puts a cinnabar square in Sam's chart (Fire) directly beside a wordmark whose one cinnabar square means "this is you."

The semantic-palette claim — "the colour alone tells you where you are" — breaks on the one case it must handle. Options: give "now" a non-colour signal (rule weight, a mono `NOW` stamp, position), or shift the accent off the Fire hex. Either is fine; leaving both is not, because it undermines the strongest idea in the identity.

## 7. Smaller things, in one list

- **The reflection question breaks the contrast rule it was given.** §4 says cinnabar is "never" used for body copy and is fine "at 24px+." `03-card-daily-note.jpg` and `07-product-screens.jpg` set the italic reflection question in cinnabar at body size. For what it's worth the rule was over-cautious — I measured `#BE4127` on `#EFEDE4` at **4.50:1**, which clears WCAG AA for normal text. But on `--paper-sunk` `#E5E2D6` it drops to **4.07:1** and fails. Either relax the rule to "cinnabar body text only on `--paper`, never on `--paper-sunk`," or fix the assets. Right now the doc and the assets disagree.
- **Two phase colours fail AA as text on paper**: `--metal` `#7C8A91` at **3.03:1** and `--earth` `#AD8449` at **2.90:1**. Fine as fills, not fine for the mono phase labels if those are ever set in phase colour. Worth one line in §4.
- **Water and Ink are nearly the same value**: `--water` `#26404F` against `--ink` `#171A18` is **1.61:1**. The doc anticipates this ("just enough blue to separate") but 1.61:1 is not separation at 12px squares. Practical consequence: a Water-heavy user's personal mark is close to indistinguishable from the all-ink brand-neutral mark, which weakens the "every user's mark is different" promise for exactly the users whose charts run cold.
- **Three different formulations of the same idea across three surfaces**: "a field guide to your own conditions" (doc §0), "a field guide to your own weather" (`02-identity-board.jpg`), "an almanac for a person" (§8 positioning). For a system this disciplined about single accents and single metaphors, pick one and demote the others.
- **`05-card-between.jpg` centres the two chart grids**, against §7's "Nothing is centered except the mark." Minor, but it is the kind of rule that erodes from the top.

## 8. What I would not change

Named so the criticism above is readable as proportionate:

- The solar-term date stamp. Strongest, least copyable element in the system, and it teaches for free.
- The semantic five-phase palette, once the cinnabar collision is resolved. It is the only palette in this category doing work rather than decoration.
- The eight-square personal mark. Best structural idea here — infinite, non-arbitrary, unmistakably one brand. It deserves promotion to a share surface, not demotion.
- Duotone matter photography over sky. Correct on metaphor grounds (the phases are terrestrial) and correct on competitive grounds (the sky is taken).
- No sans-serif. It is a position, it is defended, and it is why the screens do not look like every other app in the category.
- "Your chart is eight characters. We'll spend a year explaining them, two minutes at a time."
