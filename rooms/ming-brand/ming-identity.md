## 0. The one idea everything hangs off

**BaZi is not an oracle. It is a calendar.**

Four Pillars is the oldest continuously used system for describing *conditions* — sixty-year stem-and-branch cycles, twenty-four solar terms, five phases of matter. It was farmers' infrastructure before it was anybody's mysticism. That is the fact the whole brand is built on, because it does three jobs at once:

1. It is the honest description of the system, so we never have to bluff.
2. It is structurally the opposite of fortune-telling, which is the founder's hard constraint.
3. It is *deeper* than Western astrology in a way a stranger can feel in three seconds, without a single mystical signal.

So: **MING is an almanac, not an oracle.**

One formulation, not three. "An almanac for a person" is the line. "Field guide to your own conditions / weather" is retired — it was the same idea in a weaker coat, and a system this strict about single accents does not get three ways to say its own premise.

**The word has to be taught, not assumed.** In English the reference almanac is The Old Farmer's Almanac, which sells long-range weather prediction and [advertises an 80% hit rate](https://www.almanac.com/how-accurate-old-farmers-almanacs-weather-forecast) that [independent study puts nearer 51%](https://www.cleveland.com/metro/2026/07/how-accurate-is-the-old-farmers-almanac-independent-study-found-about-51-accuracy.html), by a method that [reportedly includes astrology](https://www.familyhandyman.com/article/farmers-almanac-accuracy/). So the word we chose to prove we don't forecast, forecasts. We keep it anyway — it is the right shape and there is no better one — and we defuse it by never letting the claim travel alone: **wherever "almanac" appears, the solar-term stamp appears with it.** `白露 · WHITE DEW · DAY 3` is the demonstration. It says today's name, not tomorrow's weather, and it settles the meaning faster than any sentence about it could.

Every decision below is downstream of that sentence. If a choice doesn't serve it, it's out.

The name supports it. 明 is 日 + 月 — sun and moon, the two cycles that make a calendar — and it means *clear*, not *fated*. We lean on that reading and never on 命 (fate). If anyone asks what MING means: "clear." Never "destiny."

## 1. What MING is

- **An almanac for a person.** The old almanac told you what the season was doing. MING tells you what your season is doing.
- **Concrete.** Wood, fire, earth, metal, water. Months, hours, cracks, edges, damp. Materials, not vibes.
- **Two minutes, daily, forever.** The product is a habit, not a reading. The identity has to survive 365 repetitions a year without getting tiring or getting loud.
- **Conditions and choices.** Observation → theme → action → question. Every asset should be legible as some version of that shape.
- **Explanatory.** We show the mechanism. "Your day pillar is Yang Fire, and this is a Water month" is a *reason*, and reasons are what separate us from a horoscope.
- **Culturally specific without being costume.** The vocabulary — solar terms, phases, pillars — is real and stays in Chinese-plus-translation. The aesthetics are ours, not a museum's.

## 2. What MING is explicitly not

Hold this list. It is the actual working part of the identity.

**Not a fortune-telling site.** No purple-to-black gradients, star fields, glowing orbs, gold foil, dragons, taiji symbols, zodiac animals, coins, incense, crystals, tarot, or brush calligraphy used as decoration. No red-and-gold luck styling. If it would look at home on a 2009 astrology portal, it is disqualified on sight.

**Not Co-Star.** They own pure black, white Times, all-caps brutalism, cold NASA archival cutouts, and the deadpan-savage one-liner. We are their inverse on purpose: warm paper instead of void, pigment instead of monochrome, terrestrial instead of celestial, precise-and-kind instead of precise-and-cruel. Our reader already has Co-Star. We are not the funnier version of it; we are the one that explains itself.

**Not generic startup.** No Inter, no violet gradient, no rounded blob illustration, no 3D glass shapes, no "Unlock your personalized insights," no exclamation marks in product copy, no drop shadows, no gradient meshes.

**Not wellness.** No spa beige, no soft-focus hands, no "hold space," no "authentic self," no breathwork register. We are cooler and more exact than that.

**Not a prediction engine.** Never "you will," never "expect," never "lucky day," never a percentage compatibility score. The moment we predict an event we become the thing we're replacing.

But note what this rule is: **hygiene, not position.** Shipping BaZi apps already say it in their own store copy — [My BaZi](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/my-bazi-astrology/id6758286066): "effort-based language, not fate or predictions"; [Meta BaZi](https://www.metabazi.com/): "tendencies and timing, not certainties"; [Jiazi](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en&id=com.hexagramdreams.bazi) is even using our house word, "friction." Four competitors make our differentiator verbatim, so we stop selling it as one. The moat is what they cannot copy in an afternoon: **the derivation is on the page, the craft is real, and the mark is the user's own chart.** Say that instead.

**Not a diagnosis.** We describe tendencies and conditions. We never tell someone what they are, and never what's wrong with them.

**Not unaccountable.** Every note carries its derivation — the pillar, phase, and month it came from, in a line the user can check against their own chart. A brand that stakes itself on mechanism and hides the working has the same hole Co-Star does; ours is just better decorated.

## 3. Who this is for, and why the above follows

**Primary:** English-speaking, astrology-curious, roughly 22–38, already carrying Co-Star, The Pattern, CHANI, Human Design, Enneagram, attachment theory, or a journaling app. Almost no BaZi.

What they actually are: **frameworks-literate and slightly bored.** They have been through enough systems to know the shape of the con. They can smell a Barnum statement. They stayed for Co-Star because it was well written and looked like nothing else, and they drifted because after a year it never got deeper — it only got more clever. The unmet need is not more mystery. It is **a system with a mechanism**, that rewards paying attention over time.

That's why the identity is an almanac and not a cosmos: it promises structure and seasons — a thing you can learn — rather than a thing that is done to you. It's why the palette is semantic rather than decorative, why the type is a reading serif plus an instrument mono, and why the imagery is matter instead of sky. Depth has to be visible in the form, not claimed in the copy.

**Secondary:** Chinese diaspora and mixed-culture users who heard 八字 at home and were never given a version that spoke to their life. What they need is the opposite of exoticisation: real terminology, kept in Chinese, translated plainly, treated as ordinary knowledge rather than heritage cosplay. Keeping 白露 on the page next to "White Dew" is for them — and it happens to be the single most distinctive thing on the screen for everyone else.

## 4. Color

Ink on paper, plus five pigments that mean something.

The palette is **semantic before it is decorative**: the five phase colors are the data layer. A user learns them the way they learned their star sign, except these actually carry information — a Fire month renders cinnabar, a Water day renders ink-blue, and after six weeks the color alone tells you where you are. Decorative color would be a missed opportunity; this is the identity doing work.

### Base — the almanac page

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--paper` | `#EFEDE4` | Default background everywhere. Warm-grey printed paper, not spa beige, not white. |
| `--paper-raised` | `#F8F6F0` | Sheets and panels that sit above the page. |
| `--paper-sunk` | `#E5E2D6` | Recessed fields, table stripes, disabled states. |
| `--rule` | `#D6D2C4` | Hairline rules and borders. 1px, always. |
| `--ink` | `#171A18` | All primary text and the wordmark. Near-black with a faint green cast — printed ink, not `#000`. Pure black is Co-Star's; we don't use it. |
| `--ink-soft` | `#5A5F59` | Secondary text, marginalia, timestamps. |

### The five phases — the data layer

| Phase | Token | Hex | Why this hue and not the obvious one |
|---|---|---|---|
| 木 Wood | `--wood` | `#4A5D3A` | Olive-sap, not kelly green. Growing things are dusty, not saturated. |
| 火 Fire | `--fire` | `#BE4127` | Cinnabar — the pigment of seal ink. A material red, not a warning red, not a lucky red. |
| 土 Earth | `--earth` | `#AD8449` | Loess ochre, not yellow. Soil, not sunshine. |
| 金 Metal | `--metal` | `#7C8A91` | Cool grey-blue of an unpolished blade. Not gold, not silver, not chrome — gold is exactly the fortune-teller signal we're refusing. |
| 水 Water | `--water` | `#2F5468` | Deep ink-blue. In the tradition Water is black; `#26404F` sat at 1.61:1 against `--ink`, which is not separation at 12px, so it lifts to 2.16:1 and stays cold. |

### Accent

Cinnabar `#BE4127` is the accent — and it is also 火 Fire, which was a collision, because on a Fire day the "you are here" marker and the data were the same red. The data layer wins. **On any surface rendering chart data, cinnabar means Fire and nothing else.** "Now" gets a non-color signal instead: an ink-filled square with the glyph knocked out in paper, plus the mono stamp. Inversion is a printer's move and it survives a Fire month.

On marketing surfaces with no chart on them, cinnabar is the accent — one per surface, still meaning "now." The brand-neutral mark keeps its single cinnabar square. In-product, beside live chart data, the wordmark uses the all-ink mark so the logo never competes with a real Fire branch.

**Phase colors are fills, with one exception: the chart itself.** Mono phase labels, marginalia, and body copy are ink or ink-soft — `--metal` and `--earth` fail AA on paper as type, at 3.03:1 and 2.90:1. But the stem and branch glyphs *are* the data, so they carry their phase, and two of them need a deeper variant to be readable at that size:

| Type-only variant | Hex | On paper |
|---|---|---|
| `--earth-ink` | `#7E5C2C` | 5.18:1 |
| `--metal-ink` | `#586A72` | 4.81:1 |

Wood (6.14:1), Fire (4.50:1), and Water (6.92:1) set as type unchanged. Nothing outside the chart glyphs uses phase color as type. This also retires the cinnabar reflection question: the italic already marks it, and color on top of italic was the same emphasis paid for twice.

### Hard rules

- No gradient, ever. Not in backgrounds, not in buttons, not behind type. Gradients are the fortune-teller tell.
- No glow, bloom, or drop shadow. Depth comes from hairline rules and paper-tone shifts.
- No pure white and no pure black surfaces.
- Phase colors are used at full strength or at 8% tint on paper. No in-between opacities.
- A screen shows at most two phase colors plus ink, unless it is the chart itself.

## 5. Typography

Two families. No sans-serif anywhere. That is a position, not an oversight: a sans is what every other app in this category reaches for when it wants to look trustworthy, and it is exactly how you arrive at generic.

**Newsreader** — display and reading. Open-source, optical-size aware, literary without being antique. It reads as a well-set page rather than a UI, which is right for a product whose core object is a paragraph you read once a day. Not Times: Times is Co-Star's, and Newsreader is warmer, narrower, and more modern in the terminals.

**IBM Plex Mono** — labels, data, and the chart. It is the *instrument* voice: dates, solar terms, phase names, stem-and-branch characters, pillar headers. Mono in the margins is what makes the page read as a record — an almanac, a field notebook, a log — rather than a magazine. It is engineered rather than styled, which is the correct note for a system that runs on a sixty-year cycle.

```
Import: https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Newsreader:ital,opsz,wght@0,6..72,200..500;1,6..72,200..500&family=IBM+Plex+Mono:wght@400;500&display=swap
```

| Role | Family / setting |
|---|---|
| Display | Newsreader 300, 44–72px, line-height 1.05, tracking −0.01em |
| Headline | Newsreader 400, 26–34px, line-height 1.2 |
| Body | Newsreader 400, 17px, line-height 1.65, measure 60–66 characters |
| Reading emphasis | Newsreader 400 *italic*, in `--ink` — reserved for the daily reflection question, nothing else |
| Label / stamp | IBM Plex Mono 500, 11–13px, UPPERCASE, tracking +0.12em |
| Data / chart | IBM Plex Mono 400–500, tabular |

**Rules.** Mono never sets body copy. Newsreader never goes below 200 or above 500 — no bold; emphasis is size, space, and color. Italic is reserved for the reflection question so the question is recognisable from across the room. One display line per screen. Measure never exceeds 66 characters, because the daily note is meant to be read, not skimmed.

## 6. The mark

There is no drawn logo, and there shouldn't be one. 八字 means *eight characters*. The mark is the eight characters.

**The eight-square mark:** a 4 × 2 grid of small squares. Four columns = the four pillars (year, month, day, hour), read right to left. Top row = the heavenly stems, bottom row = the earthly branches. Each square carries its phase color.

No 明 pun. 明 is a left–right compound, [⿰日月](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%98%8E) — never stacked; the stacked oracle-bone form is a different character, 朙. "Sun over moon" was wrong, it is printed on `01-mark-system`, and it is exactly the error a diaspora reader screenshots back at us. Stems above branches is the correct reason for the geometry and it never needed a second one. 明 = 日 + 月 stays where it belongs, in the name.

Which makes the mark **personal by construction: every user's mark is a different eight colors.** Their chart *is* the logo. That gives us an infinite, non-arbitrary asset system — share cards, profile marks, comparison views — where every variant is self-evidently the same brand because the geometry never changes and the palette is closed.

- Brand-neutral version (marketing, app icon, favicon): all eight squares in `--ink` on `--paper`, except the day-pillar stem square in `--fire`. That one cinnabar square is the "you are here."
- A personal mark never uses `--ink` for a square. Every stem and every branch has a phase, so all eight are always pigment. Ink squares mean "this is the logo, not a person."
- Squares are hard-edged, 2px radius maximum, separated by a 1px paper gutter. Never rounded into dots, never outlined, never animated with a shimmer.

### The mark is the share surface

Co-Star's distribution mechanic was cruelty-as-screenshot — a [confirmed meme format](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/co-star-notifications) built on notifications users enjoyed being [dragged by](https://theoutline.com/post/7326/my-astrology-app-hates-me-and-i-can-t-get-enough). We ban that mechanic in §8 and we owe a replacement, because a product whose invite loop is chart comparison cannot have no reason to be posted.

The replacement is the mark itself, and it is better than a rude notification because it is *yours*. Spec it as a real surface, not an internal note:

- **Export card.** The eight squares large, the eight characters set in mono beneath them, the solar-term stamp, the wordmark small. No copy from the note — the reading is private, the chart is not.
- **Profile mark.** The bare grid, legible at 88px and at 24px, no wordmark. This is the avatar.
- **Why it travels:** unique per person, unmistakably one brand, and completely opaque to anyone who hasn't downloaded the app. It asks a question rather than delivering a punchline, which is the same job Co-Star's notifications did without the cost we refused to pay.

**Wordmark:** MING in Newsreader 300, uppercase, tracking +0.18em, in `--ink`. Sits beneath the mark at the same optical width, or to its right on a shared baseline. Never on a colored field, never in cinnabar, never with a tagline locked to it.

## 7. Imagery

Two layers only. Nothing else is on-brand.

**Layer one — matter.** Documentary macro photographs of the five phases as physical material: split pine end-grain, banked embers under ash, cracked clay, an unpolished blade edge, still deep water. Flat overcast light, straight-on, frame-filling, unstyled, no hands, no faces, no props. Each one is then printed as a **duotone in `--paper` and that phase's pigment** — which is what guarantees brand recognition across assets regardless of the source photograph, and which reads as risograph/almanac-plate rather than stock.

Why this and not a starfield: the sky is the competitor's territory and the wrong metaphor besides. BaZi's five phases are terrestrial and seasonal — they are about damp, heat, dryness, cold, and what grows. Photographing actual matter is the most direct possible statement that this system is about your conditions, not your stars. It is also the one image direction in this category that a stranger cannot mistake for anything else on their phone.

**Layer two — the grid.** The chart itself, set large, as the graphic. Eight characters in a strict typographic grid, hairline rules, mono labels, phase color. Type-as-image. When an asset needs a hero and a photograph would be too soft, the answer is always the grid.

**Never:** stars, constellations, planets, nebulae, moons, zodiac animals, taiji, hexagrams as ornament, tarot, crystals, incense, hands cradling light, silhouettes against a sunset, gradient blobs, 3D glass, isometric illustration, smiling stock people, AI-slop faces.

**Layout.** The almanac page: generous outer margin, one column of text at 60–66 characters, hairline horizontal rules, and marginalia in mono — date, solar term, day count — sitting in the outer margin like an almanac header. Nothing is centered except the mark — including chart grids, which sit left on the rule like everything else. Nothing floats.

## 8. Voice

### The speaker

**A knowledgeable friend reading the weather with you.** Someone who actually knows the system, is unhurried, tells you the mechanism, and then leaves the choice with you. Warm, but not soft. Exact, but not cold. Never impressed with itself.

Explicitly not: the horoscope (vague, flattering, predictive), the coach (imperative, motivational), the therapist (careful, processual, "hold space"), the oracle (portentous), the sassy AI (Co-Star's deadpan cruelty). Each of those is a real temptation in this category and each one costs us the founder's constraint.

### Grammar of the voice

- **Conditions, then consequence, then choice.** "The conditions are X. That makes Y easier and Z harder. Here's one thing to do with it."
- **"Tends to," never "will."** No prediction of events, ever. No "expect."
- Second person, present tense, active voice.
- Short sentences. One idea each. Average under twenty words.
- Concrete nouns. If a sentence would survive being pasted into any other app's horoscope, it is a Barnum statement and it gets cut.
- **Show the mechanism.** Name the pillar, the phase, the month. The reason is the product.
- One metaphor per screen, maximum, and it comes from weather, seasons, materials, or craft. Never from space, magic, or light.
- The reflection question is answerable in one sentence and is never rhetorical.
- No emoji. No exclamation marks. No ALL-CAPS for emphasis. No em-dash pile-ups.
- **No template sentences.** If a line's shape survives swapping its nouns — "whether that shows up as X or as Y depends on what you put around it" — it is a mad-lib, and the second time a user meets it the mechanism claim dies with it. One line, one construction, never reused.
- **Never assert a fact about the user's week.** "Three heavy weeks in a row" is a claim the system cannot make. State the condition — third week of a Water month on a cold chart — and let the user supply the heaviness or not.

**The aphorism tier.** Two of the best headlines in the v0.9 assets are aphoristic and mechanism-free at the point of impact, which is Co-Star's register, and pretending otherwise was the weakest thing in v0.9. So it is allowed, once, with a rule: **a headline may run ahead of its mechanism only if the mechanism is on the same surface, within two lines of it.** A cryptic line that is never paid off is a horoscope. A cryptic line followed by "Metal month, Metal chart" is a hook with a receipt.

**Banned words:** destiny, fate, the universe, energy (meaning vibes), vibration, abundance, manifest, align/alignment, journey, blessed, cosmic, divine, sacred, unlock, reveal, lucky, unlucky, "the stars," "meant to be," "trust the process," "hold space," "authentic self."

**House words:** conditions, tendency, easier / harder, season, month, pillar, phase, grain, friction, ballast, weight, edge, damp, dry, room, pace.

### Naming (confirmed by zhihao feng, 2026-09-01)

The three areas of the product:

- **Chart** — understanding yourself. Not Grain. Grain was a Wood metaphor naming the self section inside a five-phase system, which quietly privileges one phase in the one place we claim the phases carry meaning; and a nav label is wayfinding, where metaphors are useless. Metaphors belong in sentences, not signage — "grain" stays a house word in copy. The tab says `CHART`; the page it opens says "Your eight characters," which teaches 八字 at the moment the user is looking at it.
- **Season** — your timing. Already the right word; the product's whole timing model is literally the 24 solar terms.
- **Overlap** — chart comparison. Not Between: [Between](https://between.us/?lang=en) is a live VCNC couples app claiming 35M couples on [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=kr.co.vcnc.android.couple) and the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/between-couples-love-tracker/id458035189), sitting in our adjacent category and owning between.us — and a preposition is unsearchable besides. Overlap keeps the original reasoning (the space, not a score), is geometric rather than romantic, and stays true for a parent, a co-founder, or a friend, which "Between" only half did.

The daily ritual is **the note**. "Today's note is up." Two minutes.

Date stamps everywhere use the solar term: `白露 · WHITE DEW · DAY 3`. This is the deepest, least-copyable, most immediately distinctive thing available to us, it is authentic to the system, and it teaches the user the calendar for free.

### Before / after

**1. A horoscope-style daily line → MING**

> **Before:** "Mercury retrograde is scrambling your communication today, Scorpio. Expect misunderstandings with someone close — but the universe is teaching you patience. Trust the process. ✨"

> **After:** "You're in a Metal month, and your chart already leans Metal. That's a lot of edge — you cut to the point faster than people expect, and it lands as blunt more often than it usually does. Today: say the hard thing, then ask one question and actually wait for the answer. *Where did you mistake being clear for being kind?*"

Why: it names the mechanism (Metal on Metal), states a tendency rather than an event, gives one action small enough to do today, and ends on a question you can answer in a sentence. Nothing is predicted and nothing is flattered.

**2. Prediction → conditions**

> **Before:** "This week brings unexpected money luck."
> **After:** "財 Wealth month: the month's branch feeds your day master. It doesn't hand you money — it makes money the easy subject to raise rather than the awkward one. If there's a rate, a raise, or a number you've been circling, this is the cheap month to say it out loud."

Note on 財 months: "more likely to go somewhere" was a probability claim about future events, which is a prediction with a Chinese label on it. The carve-out for the Ten Gods is that we describe **what the month makes easy or awkward to do**, never what it makes likely to happen.

**3. Fatalism → agency**

> **Before:** "You're a Yang Fire Day Master, so you're a natural leader."
> **After:** "Your day pillar is Yang Fire, with no Water anywhere to bank it. Fire like that starts things at full brightness and has nothing holding the temperature down afterwards. The ballast has to come from outside the chart — which, in practice, means from what you build around yourself."

**4. Compatibility score → real friction**

> **Before:** "You and Sam are 87% compatible!"
> **After:** "Your charts clash on the Earth branch — the thing you both quietly want to be in charge of. It isn't a verdict, it's a known friction. Worth naming out loud before it becomes a Thursday."

**5. Generic startup → MING**

> **Before:** "Unlock your personalized insights with our AI-powered engine."
> **After:** "Your chart is eight characters. We'll spend a year explaining them, two minutes at a time."

**6. Wellness → MING**

> **Before:** "Take a moment to breathe into your authentic self and hold space for whatever is arising."
> **After:** "Third week of a Water month, on a chart that already runs cold. Water on Water is damp and slow — it is the season doing it, not you failing at it. Nothing here needs deciding this week; the month turns on the 22nd."

**7. Onboarding**

> **Before:** "Enter your birth details to reveal your destiny."
> **After:** "Birth date, time, and place. Time matters most: it sets the hour pillar, and that's the one about how you actually operate."

**8. Notification**

> **Before:** "✨ Your daily horoscope is ready! ✨"
> **After:** "White Dew, day 3. Today's note is up."

**9. Empty state**

> **Before:** "No readings yet. Start your cosmic journey!"
> **After:** "Nothing here until you add someone. Between works by comparison — one chart on its own has nothing to be between."

**10. Paywall**

> **Before:** "Upgrade now to unlock unlimited premium insights!"
> **After:** "The free note covers today. The full chart covers your decades — including the ones you've already lived. Start there: read 2014 to 2024 and see whether it describes the years you actually had."

Note on 大運: "the next ten years" sold a forecast at the exact moment money changes hands, in a brand that says it doesn't forecast. The luck pillars stay — they are real Four Pillars and cutting them would be dishonest — but we sell them **backwards first.** The decades already lived are checkable, which makes them the only falsifiable thing in this entire category, and falsifiability is the strongest possible proof that we are not a horoscope. Forward decades are then described as conditions with dates, never as events.

### Positioning line

> **MING — an almanac for a person.**

Supporting line: *Four Pillars, in plain English. Your conditions, daily, in two minutes.*

Not a tagline lockup — it lives in body copy and app-store text, not under the wordmark.

## 9. Quick test before shipping anything

1. Cover the wordmark. Is it still obviously MING? If not, the identity isn't doing its job.
2. Could this line be pasted into any other astrology app unchanged? Then it's Barnum — cut it.
3. Does it predict an event? Cut it.
4. Is there a mechanism on the page — a pillar, a phase, a month? If not, we're a horoscope.
5. Is there more than one accent color, or any gradient? Fix it.
6. Would this have looked at home on an astrology website in 2009? Start over.
7. Swap the nouns in the strongest sentence. Does it still work? Then it's a template, not a reading.
8. Does the surface say where the reading came from — pillar, phase, month? If a user can't check it against their own chart, we're asking for trust we said we wouldn't ask for.
9. Would anyone post this? If nothing on the surface is theirs alone, it isn't a share asset.
